# Template — Layer 1 entity page

Per-entity page generated by Ingest (Workflow 3). Lives at
`01-canonical/entities/{{SLUG}}.md`. One entity per file.

An **entity** is a named real-world *thing that has identity* and
that the case interacts with: a person, an organisation, a team, a
legal entity, a product, a system, a project, a regulator, a
jurisdiction. Entities are distinct from concepts (mechanisms, KPIs,
terms of art, clauses) — both deserve their own pages, but the
shapes differ. See SKILL.md for the entity-vs-concept decision tree.

The slug is the filename without `.md`, lowercase, hyphenated.
Examples: `acme-corp`, `example-llc`, `jane-smith`, `regulatory-order-2024`.

Section headings below are English by default. If `CLAUDE.md`
section 3 declares the case is in another language, translate the
headings consistently across all entity pages and use the translated
form thereafter.

## Frontmatter notes

- `kind: entity` — always literal. Distinguishes entity pages from
  concept pages, which use `kind: concept`. Lint relies on this.
- `entity_type` — one of the listed values. Lets lint and queries
  filter by entity kind ("show me all advisors", "list all legal
  entities").
- `first_seen` — date this entity first entered the Cortex. Do not
  update on later edits; that's what the Change Log is for.
- `sources` — list of pointer IDs the entity is anchored on. Add to
  the list when later ingests touch the same entity; do not replace.
- `aliases` — alternative names, abbreviations, predecessor names.
  Lint uses this to detect duplicate entity pages.
- `confidentiality` — propagated from the strictest source touching
  the entity.
- `status` — entities can become inactive (former employees,
  dissolved entities, retired systems). Mark them inactive rather
  than deleting them; the case history may still reference them.

## When to create an entity page

- A named **person** the case interacts with (client, counterparty,
  advisor, witness, internal stakeholder with a meaningful role).
- A named **organisation** (firm, agency, supplier, subsidiary, peer
  comparator).
- A named **legal entity** (specific named legal entity by its full
  registered name).
- A named **product or system** with identity (a specific software
  product, a specific contract, a specific tender, a specific
  dataset).
- A named **regulator or jurisdiction** when its identity matters
  (a specific regulatory body by name — not generic "the
  regulator").

If you find yourself writing about something that *acts* in the
case — makes decisions, signs documents, files motions, owns
budgets — it's an entity. If you find yourself writing about
something that *defines a mechanism* — a calculation, a rule, a
metric, a clause — it's a concept.

---

## Entity vs. concept — quick test

Ask: *can this thing make a phone call?*

- Yes → entity (a person, an organisation, a regulator).
- No, but does it *do* something in the world with a name? — system,
  product, jurisdiction. Still an entity if it has a name and
  identity.
- No, it just *describes how something works* — concept.

Edge cases:

- A specific named KPI or formula → **concept** (a calculation
  rule).
- A specific named legal entity → **entity** (it owns assets, signs
  contracts).
- A specific regulation by number/name → **entity** (it acts in the
  world once issued); the regulatory *regime* (e.g. a directive
  class, a reporting standard) → **concept**.
- A specific tender → **entity**; its evaluation rubric → **concept**.
- A specific contract → **entity**; a clause within it → **concept**.

When still uncertain, prefer to err toward entity. Mis-routed pages
are cheap to fix later; lint catches them.

---

## When NOT to create an entity page

- One-off mentions of a person who never reappears.
- Generic role descriptions ("the auditor", "the regulator") without
  a specific named referent — those are concept-shaped, not
  entity-shaped.
- Synonyms or short aliases of an existing entity (use `aliases:`).
- Meeting notes, decisions, or events (those are Layer 2).
